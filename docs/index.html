Discs you can still burn, check and read in ten years.

Krouton builds and burns CDs, DVDs and Blu-ray discs on a Mac, then reads every sector back to prove the disc matches. When a Mac can no longer drive a burner, a Raspberry Pi or any spare computer can do it for you.

In development: the source is on GitHub, with no release yet

What Krouton does

Builds the disc

Data discs readable on Mac, Windows and Linux, audio CDs with CD-Text and gapless tracks, and DVD-Video, including shrinking a dual-layer DVD onto a single-layer one with its menus intact.

Proves the burn

After writing, Krouton reads the disc back and compares it with the image byte for byte. A disc that only looks finished is reported as a failure.

Rescues old discs

Copies scratched and failing discs sector by sector, coming back to unreadable areas with repeated attempts, and grades how well a disc is holding up so you know which to copy first.

Burns a set

Splits a large folder across as many discs as it needs and burns them on every drive you have at once. A disc that fails is the only one burned again.

Burning on another computer

Apple has been retiring its disc frameworks one at a time. Krouton Server is our answer: a small program for a Raspberry Pi, a Linux box, a NAS, a Windows PC or an older Mac with a burner plugged in. Pair it once with a six-digit code, then choose it like any other drive. It can also build the disc from a folder, so a device that can’t make disc images itself can still burn.

The optical-disc archive

The specifications and open-source tools that make burning possible are scattered across standards bodies, old university servers and mailing lists. We keep copies of everything we’re allowed to keep, and links to everything else, at archive.krouton.app.
